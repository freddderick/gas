    What this can and cannot do
    
    
    GazeFix measures where each eye is pointing, works out where it would have to point to
      meet the lens, and warps the eye region to close the gap. Because it rearranges the
      pixels that are already there rather than redrawing the eye, the size of the correction
      is bounded by how much of the eyeball is actually visible between the lids.
    
    
    In practice that means it handles the everyday case well — someone reading from a
      screen just below or beside the camera — and cannot manufacture eye contact from a
      profile view or from a glance right off the side of the frame. Corrections it cannot pay
      for are reported rather than faked.
